My grandson Eli was six when he challenged me to a race across the backyard. I said “sure, buddy.” I made it halfway before I had to stop, hands on my knees, chest burning, legs like wet sand.

He kept running. He didn’t even notice I’d quit. But my son did. He gave me a look I’ll never forget — the look you give your dad when you realize he’s getting old.

I was 55. I’d been pulling wire in attics and crawling under houses for thirty years. I used to be the strongest guy on every crew. And somewhere along the way, without noticing, I’d become the guy who sits down first.

Carol and I have been married 31 years. She’s the best thing that ever happened to me. But I’d started going to bed early on purpose, so I wouldn’t have to face the fact that I didn’t feel like much of a husband anymore. She’d reach for my hand on the couch and I’d pretend to be focused on the TV.

One night she asked, very quietly, “Is it me?”

It wasn’t her. It was never her. And that was the night I made the appointment.

“I wasn’t falling apart. I was just slowly running out of power — and pretending I wasn’t.”

What my doctor explained in ten minutes

Dr. Alvarez has been my doctor for fifteen years and he doesn’t sugarcoat things. He ran bloodwork and checked my blood pressure. Then he sat down and drew two circles on the back of a prescription pad.

The “two engines” he drew for me

He said a man’s energy and vitality run on two engines that both slow down with age. One is hormonal: testosterone naturally drifts lower year after year. The other is circulation: healthy blood flow is what carries oxygen and nutrients to your muscles and everywhere else they’re needed.

My numbers weren’t alarming, but both engines were running weak. His advice: walk 30 minutes a day, get my sleep back, cut down on fried food and beer, and make sure I was getting nutrients that support healthy hormone levels and circulation. Then come back in three months.

I remember thinking: so it’s not just “getting old.” It’s two specific things. And I can do something about both.

Why most “quick fixes” only work on half the problem

Engine one: hormones

Stress, poor sleep and age all chip away at healthy testosterone. Most energy drinks and “men’s pills” never touch this. They just spike you with caffeine and drop you an hour later.

Engine two: circulation

Your body uses nitric oxide to help blood vessels relax and keep blood moving. When circulation slows, you feel it as fatigue, cold extremities and lower stamina.

That’s why nothing I’d tried before had worked. I’d been throwing caffeine at a problem that needed nutrition — on both sides.
